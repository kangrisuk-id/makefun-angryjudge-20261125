# Candidates — Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest

Brief recap: one short funny rant about a small everyday annoyance, ≤400 characters,
written to `/output/entry.txt`. The judge (furious, unimpressed) compares entries
pairwise on, in order: (1) it is a funny rant about an everyday annoyance, not an
essay or a list; (2) actually funny — a real laugh; (3) specific, concrete
annoyance; (4) sharp, economical wording, no throat-clearing; (5) freshness —
an angle not heard a hundred times. Length for its own sake does not count.

Scoring below is mine, per candidate, 1–5 on the judge's four scorable axes:
**F** funny, **S** specific, **E** economical, **N** fresh (new angle).
Total = F+S+E+N (max 20). The top 8 by total go into a pairwise tournament
run head-to-head in the judge's own order of criteria.

## Tournament (top 8 by score, judge's criteria order applied head-to-head)

Seeds: 11 CAPTCHA (19), 13 Wobbly cart (19), 1 Self-checkout (18), 37 Do-not-reply (18),
2 Printer actor (17), 4 Courier (17), 15 Three alarms (17), 19 Verification code (17).
(Several other 17s — 3, 5, 16, 22, 25, 27, 28, 33, 35, 36, 38 — lost the seed
tie-break on freshness, then economy.)

Quarter-finals:
- 11 vs 19: both specific and fresh; 11's premise inverts the whole robot
  takeover story and ends on a bigger laugh. **11 wins.**
- 13 vs 15: 15 is tighter, but 13's wheel-as-witness is the fresher image and
  the laugh is bigger. **13 wins.**
- 1 vs 2: 2 is charming but softer; 1's anger feels shared — everyone has been
  treated as a bag criminal. **1 wins.**
- 37 vs 4: 37 is the most economical text in the field, but it is one joke;
  4 builds three escalating beats and the 'deliberately, with paperwork'
  closer lands hardest. **4 wins.**

Semi-finals:
- 11 vs 13: both 19s. 13 is a single sustained metaphor; 11 has a premise,
  a humiliation and a reversal ('the robots are waiting for me to finish the
  test'). On criterion 2 — the bigger laugh — **11 wins narrowly.**
- 1 vs 4: 4 is longer and its length earns it, but 1 is sharper per word and
  'Deborah' plus the criminal-career closer is the most quotable line here.
  **1 wins narrowly.**

Final:
- 11 vs 1: both pass criterion 1 easily. On funny: 1 gets a strong laugh from
  recognition; 11 gets recognition plus surprise — the freshest angle in the
  field (criterion 5 breaks it, and criterion 2 is at least level). The judge
  punishes anything it has heard a hundred times; bag-check rants are a known
  genre, CAPTCHA role-reversal is not. **Winner: 11 — CAPTCHA traffic lights.**
